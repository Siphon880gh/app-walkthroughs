## Design Context

### Users
StoryFlow Studio is a desktop-first walkthrough engineering workbench for product managers and UX leads, product and interaction designers, frontend engineers and QA specialists, and customer-facing teams. They use it during feature kickoffs, design handoffs, bug reproduction, stakeholder reviews, and onboarding. Their central job is to turn loose screenshots into a precise, sequenced explanation of what a user does, what the interface shows, and what should happen next—then play or export that flow without accounts, backend infrastructure, or cloud uploads.

### Brand Personality
Focused, precise, and technically credible. The interface should make users feel in control of a complex flow, confident that details will survive handoff, and calm during sustained analytical work. Copy is concise and operational rather than playful. StoryFlow Studio behaves like a trustworthy instrument panel whose purpose is to illuminate the user's screenshots and decisions.

### Aesthetic Direction
Use the established “Avionics Cockpit” direction: a dark-mode-only, high-density workspace built from deep midnight slate surfaces, razor-thin architectural dividers, electric-blue active states, and restrained semantic accents. Pair Plus Jakarta Sans for interface text with JetBrains Mono for counters, coordinates, timings, and telemetry. Favor tonal layering, compact controls, precise device frames, and generous stage space around screenshot content. Avoid decorative gradients, generic card grids, excessive glow or glass effects, playful illustrations, light surfaces, and ornamental motion that competes with the work.

### Design Principles
1. Screenshots are the illuminated artifacts; chrome stays quiet, compact, and structurally clear.
2. Make behavior explicit through the canonical triad: user action, visible screen state, and next action.
3. Expose measurable implementation details—coordinates, duration, easing, dwell, and device frame—at the point of use.
4. Preserve local-first trust: interactions should feel immediate, portable, and independent of remote services.
5. Use color and motion only to communicate state, category, selection, or transition; never as decoration.
6. Maintain keyboard access, visible focus, high contrast, reduced-motion support, and responsive fallbacks without removing core functionality.
